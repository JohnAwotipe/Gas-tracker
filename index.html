🔥 Gas Usage Tips

To help you get the most accurate gas consumption date, please keep track of how long you spend cooking each meal.

Try not to cook food for longer than the recommended cooking time.

For better gas management, use medium or low heat whenever possible.

Keep in mind that higher heat uses more gas, which can make your gas finish earlier than the expected date.

Your cooking habits may change from week to week. For example, you may cook more or use more gas in the second week than in the first week. This can affect the accuracy of your estimated gas consumption date.

WHY ESTIMATES CAN CHANGE

Your gas usage is personal.

Two households can use the same cylinder size and finish their gas on different dates. Cooking time, heat level, meal choices, number of meals, and changes in routine can all affect consumption.

That is why this tracker is designed as a planning tool, not a physical gas-level sensor. The more consistent your cooking habits are, the more useful your estimate can become.

FAQ

Questions people often ask

Does the tracker physically measure the gas inside my cylinder?

No. It estimates your expected consumption date using the dates and gas information you enter. It does not measure the physical gas level inside the cylinder.

Can my gas finish earlier than the date shown?

Yes. The date is an estimate. Heavy cooking, high heat, longer cooking times, or a change in your routine can make gas finish earlier than expected.

What does “Prepare to refill your cooking gas” mean?

It is an early reminder. It means your expected consumption date is getting close, so you can plan your refill instead of waiting until the gas runs out.

What does “It's time to refill your cooking gas” mean?

It means today is the expected consumption date you entered. You should check your cylinder and plan to refill it if necessary.

Can I use the tracker again after refilling?

Yes. After each refill, enter your new refill date and new expected consumption date. Your previous entry can remain in your refill history for reference.

GAS SAFETY CENTER

Know the warning signs and stay safe

These are general safety and maintenance tips for common cooking-gas problems. When you are unsure, stop using the appliance and contact a qualified gas technician or your gas supplier.

1. How can I know if my gas is leaking?

Never use a match or lighter to test for a leak. If you suspect a leak, soapy water can be applied around a suspected connection or valve area. Continuous bubbles can indicate escaping gas.

  • Turn off the gas supply if it is safe.
  • Keep flames, cigarettes and sparks away.
  • Do not switch electrical appliances on or off near the suspected leak.
  • Move to a safe, well-ventilated place and contact a qualified gas professional.

2. What if fire is coming from the control handle?

Stop using the cooker immediately. A flame around the control handle or valve area can indicate that gas is escaping and should not be treated as a simple cleaning problem.

  • Turn off the gas supply if it is safe to do so.
  • Keep away from the flame and do not continue cooking.
  • Allow the appliance to cool.
  • Have the burner, valve and connections inspected before using it again.

3. Why is my burner flame very low?

Food particles, grease, oil or other residue can block the small burner openings. This may cause a weak or uneven flame, including a flame that appears on only part of the burner.

  • Turn off the gas and let the burner cool.
  • Clean it according to the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Make sure the burner is completely dry before use.
  • If the problem remains, have it checked by a qualified technician or replace the burner if necessary.

4. Is a rusty cylinder with no stand safe?

It depends on the cylinder's condition. Severe rust, deep corrosion, serious dents or other visible damage should not be ignored. Have the cylinder inspected by a qualified gas professional or replace it when necessary.

  • Keep the cylinder upright and stable.
  • Protect it from unnecessary moisture and conditions that encourage corrosion.
  • Do not take chances with a badly damaged cylinder.
🛑 SAFETY FIRST: If you smell gas strongly, suspect a serious leak, or see a flame where it should not be, move away from the danger area and seek professional help. Do not create sparks or flames while investigating a suspected leak.
COOKING GAS GUIDE

Understand your cooking gas, plan better, and reduce surprises

Cooking Gas Tracker is designed to help you plan around your expected gas consumption. A reminder is useful because cooking gas does not always run out on the same day for every household. Your cooking routine, the meals you prepare, how long you cook, and the amount of heat you use can all affect how quickly a cylinder is consumed.

The guide below explains how to use the tracker responsibly, what your cylinder size means, why estimates can change, and some practical ways to manage everyday cooking gas use.

01

How cooking gas consumption works

LPG consumption is closely connected to how much heat you use over time. A meal that needs a longer cooking period can use more gas than a meal that finishes quickly. Regular cooking throughout the day can also produce a different consumption pattern from occasional cooking.

For this reason, a cylinder should not be treated as having one guaranteed number of days for every household. Two homes using the same cylinder size can have different refill dates because their cooking habits are different.

02

Why your gas may finish earlier than expected

Your expected consumption date is a planning estimate, not a measurement of the physical gas remaining inside the cylinder. The estimate can change when your normal routine changes.

  • You cook more meals than usual.
  • Cooking takes longer than normal.
  • You frequently use high heat when medium heat would be enough.
  • You prepare meals that require long boiling, frying, or simmering.
  • More people are eating at home or your household routine changes.
03

How to make your gas usage more predictable

Consistency makes a consumption estimate more useful. After a refill, try to keep a simple record of your cooking routine and compare your actual refill date with the date you expected.

If you repeatedly finish earlier or later than your previous estimate, adjust the next expected consumption date based on your real experience. The tracker becomes more useful when the information you enter reflects your normal household routine.

04

Use the right heat for the meal

More flame does not automatically mean food will cook better or finish sooner. Once a meal is already cooking effectively, using an unnecessarily high flame can waste heat around the cookware.

Where appropriate for the recipe, use a steady medium or low heat and avoid leaving a burner running when it is not needed. Follow the cooking instructions for the particular food rather than using maximum heat by default.

05

Prepare food efficiently

Simple preparation habits can help you avoid unnecessary burner time. Have ingredients ready before turning on the stove, use cookware that suits the burner, and keep lids on pots when the recipe allows it.

Keeping cooking times under control also makes your personal consumption pattern easier to understand. The goal is not to rush food; it is to avoid spending extra time and heat when it is not necessary.

06

Keep a refill history

Your refill history can help you see your household's pattern over time. Record the date you refill, the amount of gas you purchased, and the expected consumption date.

Looking back at several refill periods is more useful than relying on one unusually short or unusually long period. Your routine can change, so treat history as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Important: Cooking Gas Tracker does not physically weigh, sense, or measure the LPG inside your cylinder. It uses the information you provide to help you plan around an expected consumption date. You may still have gas remaining when a reminder appears, and your gas can also finish before the estimated date.

Understanding LPG cylinder sizes

Cylinder size refers to the amount of LPG the cylinder is designed to hold. A larger cylinder can contain more LPG than a smaller cylinder, but size alone does not tell you exactly how many days the gas will last.

Cylinder sizes and availability can vary by supplier and location. Always use the size and refill information that matches your actual cylinder and purchase.

07

How to use your expected consumption date

Choose a date based on your best understanding of your normal usage. If you are using the tracker for the first time, your first estimate may need adjustment because you may not yet have enough personal history.

When the date gets close, the reminder is a prompt to prepare. It is not a statement that the cylinder is definitely empty.

08

What the reminders mean

“Prepare to refill your cooking gas” is an early reminder that your expected consumption date is approaching. It gives you time to plan a refill.

“It's time to refill your cooking gas” means the expected consumption date you entered has arrived. Check the actual gas remaining in your cylinder and refill when necessary.

Basic cooking gas safety

Tracking your consumption is only part of responsible gas use. LPG is a fuel and should always be handled carefully. Follow the safety instructions supplied with your cylinder, regulator, hose, and cooking appliance.

  • Keep the cylinder upright and in a suitable, well-ventilated location according to the supplier's safety guidance.
  • Keep LPG cylinders and gas appliances away from unnecessary heat sources and anything that could ignite the gas.
  • Inspect the hose, regulator, and connections regularly for signs of damage or wear, and replace damaged components appropriately.
  • If you suspect a gas leak, avoid flames and do not operate electrical switches near the suspected leak. Move to a safe place and contact an appropriate qualified gas professional or emergency service.
  • Do not attempt repairs or modifications to LPG equipment unless you are properly qualified to do so.

For any immediate safety concern, follow your local emergency guidance and the instructions provided by your LPG supplier or appliance manufacturer.

A simple gas-management routine

After a refill: enter the new refill date, gas amount, and your expected consumption date. Keep the information as accurate as possible.

During the refill period: keep your cooking routine reasonably consistent and pay attention to unusually heavy cooking days.

When a reminder arrives: use it as a planning signal. Check your actual cylinder condition rather than assuming the gas is completely finished.

After the next refill: compare the actual refill date with your previous estimate. Use what you learned to make the next estimate more realistic.

This approach turns the tracker into a simple personal record rather than relying on a one-size-fits-all prediction.
